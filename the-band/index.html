      The Band

      Twenty-five people, one valley, every season.

      The real terrain of the lower Tech valley in Roussillon, 25 km across, from the Albères to the edge of the plain. A Neolithic band lives off what it can hunt and gather. Each morning they send out hunters, gatherers and wood carriers. When the land near camp is exhausted they move on, carrying only a few days of food and caching the rest.

      Pasture grows with rain and warmth. Deer, aurochs, ibex and boar follow it and keep away from people. Rain fills the streams; groundwater keeps the larger ones running through summer. Snow comes to the heights in hard winters.

      You lead them. Each morning the council asks where to send hunting, gathering, firewood and stone parties, whether to set fires, or whether to break camp. Lightning starts wildfires in the dry season; the valley is a mosaic of old burns. Stone tools wear out; new ones are knapped from stone fetched from outcrops. When you scout a new camp you see everything within a day’s round trip of it. In the evening the parties tell you what they brought back and what animals they saw.
